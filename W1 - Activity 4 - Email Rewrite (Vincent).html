Hover over the underlined words to see the feedback.

🔍 What to work on
✗ Fix these
Typo: "Coffe" → "Coffee"

Also: "Also we would like" → "We would also like to" (smoother word order)

Article + noun: "as front desk" → "as the front desk clerk" — missing article, and "clerk" specifies your individual role (not a team)

False cognate: "organigram" → "org chart" — "organigram" comes from Spanish/French (organigrama / organigramme) and is not a word in English. Native speakers won't recognize it. Always use "org chart" or "organizational chart"

Collocation: "ok for you" → "works for you" (more professional)
✓ Keep these
Structure: Subject line, greeting, body, closing — all correct.

"Last but not least": Sophisticated connector, used well.

Tone: Friendly but professional — the right register for this situation.

Purpose: Clear — you ask for something specific and explain why.
📌 Language focus — Articles & Collocations
Pattern to watch: Missing articles under slight pressure.

"as front desk" → as the front desk clerk — article + specific role noun
"an organigram" → an org chart — "organigram" is a false cognate from Spanish/French; it doesn't exist in standard English. A native speaker will not understand it.

When you're focused on content, articles tend to disappear. This is a French interference pattern — worth catching in writing before it becomes a habit.
💡 Useful collocations for professional emails:
"works for you" / "suits you" (not "is ok for you")
"the front desk clerk" / "from the front desk" (not "as front desk")
"an org chart" / "an organizational chart" (never "organigram")
"a mailing list" ✓ — this one was correct!
