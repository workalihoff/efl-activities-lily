Your Professional Bio in English

This bio is something you'll actually use — in Geneva, in email signatures, in introductions. Write it well, write it once.

🎯 Why this task
At the Geneva training, you will likely be asked to introduce yourself in writing or in person — to trainers, to colleagues from other countries, in registration forms, in shared documents. Your bio should be ready before you arrive.
💡 A professional bio is not a CV. It's a curated narrative — you choose what to include, how much detail to give, and what impression to create. Every sentence earns its place.
📋 Structure for a 150-word professional bio
1
Name + current role
One sentence. State who you are and where you work.
2
What you actually do
One or two sentences. Use "I look after..." or "I handle..." — not just your title.
3
Key achievement or background
One sentence that makes you memorable. A milestone, a specialization, or a notable project.
4
Why you're here / your goal
One sentence connecting your background to your reason for being at this training.
💬 Phrases to use — from this week's vocabulary
Describing your role
🔹 I look after [area] at [organization]
🔹 I handle [type of work], including [specific example]
🔹 I have a background in [field], which in practice means [daily work]
Describing experience
🔹 I have [X years of] experience in [area]
🔹 I have worked on [type of project/initiative]
🔹 I specialize in [area]
Connecting to the training
🔹 I am attending this training to [purpose / to + infinitive]
🔹 I look forward to [gerund] and [gerund]
🔹 I hope to [base verb] and apply [what] to [where]
⚠️ Patterns to avoid — from your output this week
❌ "I am being in charge of..."
✓ "I am in charge of..." / "I am responsible for..."
❌ "I work on Guatemala"
✓ "I work in Guatemala" / "I work on issues related to Guatemala"
❌ "She doesn't / I doesn't..."
✓ "I don't..." — third person singular -s only for he/she/it
❌ "actually" (meaning "currently")
✓ "currently" / "at present" / "right now"
✍️ Write your bio — 130 to 160 words

Write in the third person (she / Gabriela) — that's the standard format for professional bios submitted to training programs. Aim for 130–160 words. Do not use ChatGPT or AI for this task.

⚠️ Write your first draft without stopping to check grammar. Get the content right first — then review. Perfectionism is the enemy of production.

Model bio (you can use this as a reference, not as a template to copy):

Gabriela is a human rights lawyer currently based in Guatemala City, where she works as a Human Rights Officer and Civic Space Focal Point at the Office of the United Nations High Commissioner for Human Rights (OHCHR). She looks after monitoring and accountability work on human rights issues in Guatemala, with a particular focus on LGBTI rights, women's rights, and the prevention of gender-based violence from an intersectional perspective. She has been instrumental in advancing inter-institutional actions to address sexual harassment in public spaces and has contributed to the report Guatemala: Challenges in the Defence of Human Rights 2020–2025. She is attending the Advanced Training Course on the Effective Integration of Human Rights in Law Enforcement to strengthen her capacity to apply international standards in her work and contribute her field experience to the shared learning.

This is a model — yours should reflect your actual background, your voice, and your priorities. Don't copy it. Learn from its structure.
